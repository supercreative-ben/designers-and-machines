## 2. Luma workflow

**Luma is the source of truth for event management:** the event listing, applications, attendee acceptance, and scheduled blasts.

1. Duplicate the previous month's Luma event.
2. Update the description for the new month and date, and update the event's actual date settings. Keep the same time as the previous edition; check the timezone.
3. Confirm the venue and update the location, address, and entrance instructions so attendees go to the right place.
4. Copy and paste last month's scheduled blasts into the new event. Update references to the month, date, venue, entrance, and event link, and move the send schedule to the new edition's dates. Check the recipients and remove stale details before scheduling.
5. Verify the new listing and blasts agree on the date, time, and location. Record the new Luma link for the website update.

### Reviewing applications

The host and agent can use the **Luma API** to go through people who applied. Prioritize designers who are shipping really cool work that feels inspiring. Review the submitted work rather than lowering the bar to fill the room.

- **Quality over quantity:** It is completely okay to have only **20–30 accepted attendees a week before** the event. Many good people sign up at the last minute; leave room for them.
- **Agent support:** Triage applications, inspect the submitted links, and give the host recommendations with a short explanation grounded in the work. The host makes final acceptance decisions; automated acceptance or rejection is not part of this triage delegation.
- **Access:** The exact Luma organizer/API access setup still needs to be documented. The website's public spots counter does not establish applicant access.

### Agent handoff

This monthly workflow should be easy for an outbound agent to run, continuing the organizer's previous agent-assisted approach. Give the agent the previous and new Luma event links, confirmed date and venue, application criteria, blast schedule, selected demos, selected tweet URLs, and the host's instructions for sending and publishing. Keep the completed steps and unresolved items in this repo so the next host can pick up the work.

**Delegated workflow:** The agent should help triage acceptance, rewrite the copied blasts so they make sense for the new edition, automatically publish the event promotion posts, and automatically deploy the website updates. Ben has authorized automatic post publication and deployment for this event workflow; once the destination account, event details, and assets are established, a separate approval step is not needed for each post or deployment. Check the resulting live post and website after publishing. Keep final attendee decisions with the host and preserve unresolved facts as TODOs.

## 5. Run of show

### Standard schedule

Keep this timing from edition to edition. All times are local to San Francisco (`America/Los_Angeles`).

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **5:00 p.m.** | Start venue preparation, chairs, and equipment — 90 minutes before check-in. |
| **6:30–7:00 p.m.** | Welcome attendees and check everyone in. |
| **7:00 p.m.** | Doors close; demos begin. Put this cutoff in the attendee reminders. |
| **7:00–8:00 p.m.** | Demos and Q&A. |
| **8:00–9:00 / 9:30 p.m.** | Dinner and conversation. |
| **By 10:00 p.m.** | Aim to have everyone out the door. |

### Setup — 5:00 p.m.

- Re-read the notes.
- Check that everything is fine with the office manager.
- Stop at the venue and set up the chairs.

Then the mindset: *"I did my homework, I invited the right people — now the show must go on."* Put on a smile and greet people.

### Greeting attendees

Give everyone clear steps as they walk in — think of it as clear UX:

> Welcome, so cool that you're here. You can put your things there. You can talk to the person next to you — and we start in 20 minutes.

### Opening pitch — same every time

Keep the core message consistent, updating the edition number and personal introduction for the current host. Each edition, add 2–3 sentences about what makes the current moment interesting. The onboarding notes suggested a new model release opening a new field of possibility (using "Opus 5.5" as an example): "no more excuses not to build." Choose and verify a timely example before using it.

Original organizer's opening for edition #6:

> Welcome, everybody. This is the sixth Designers and Machines.
>
> I arrived in SF in January, and some of the coolest people I've met were at this event. Relationships and companies have started here.
>
> Go on [designers-machines.com](https://www.designers-machines.com/) to chat and see the other attendees.
>
> Rules of the game: 50 attendees, 300 people on the waitlist, 5 minutes each, 1 minute Q&A, dinner after.

Community framing to weave in: people have met co-founders here, started companies, even hooked up. Designers are passionate people who are tired of overly formal formats.

### Demo format

- **5-minute demos** per speaker — start clapping at 5 minutes to keep it moving.
- **1-minute Q&A** between each.
- Then a download / debrief.
- **Dinner after.**

### Selecting speakers

Open the submitted project/demo links **one by one**. Ask: **"Oh, wow, is this actually really cool?"** If the work produces that reaction, it is a strong reason to accept the person as a speaker. Prefer inspiring things people have actually shipped. Demos that feel **whimsical, a little funny, and not too serious** usually work best here. The agent can help inspect links and shortlist candidates for the host.

## 7. Community & growth

- **At least two posts per month:** Publish one recap post about the event and one hype-up post for the next edition. Include the next event's Luma link in the hype-up post.
- **Promotion format:** Include **one or two images or a short video**, and build excitement. Look at [Ben's X / Twitter](https://x.com/ben_issen) for previous hype-up and recap tweets before drafting; use their tone and format as references, updating all event-specific details. Ben's existing post URLs are also recorded in `data/events.ts`.
- **Publishing:** The agent can publish the posts automatically as part of the delegated workflow. Establish the destination posting account and exact publishing schedule during setup.
- **Site chat:** Attendees connect their X account on [designers-machines.com](https://www.designers-machines.com/) to see names and profile pictures. It is a standalone database on the site — not an X group.
- **Outreach reflex:** When you spot a great designer on Twitter, DM them, tell them their work is great, and when they are in SF invite them to the next event. It works better than "let's grab coffee" — the event is the pretext.
- **Recurring series > one-offs:** Consistent events build a community where people reliably show up and meet each other. The most important thing is to keep going and not miss an edition.

### Next-day website update

Keep each edition's content attached to the correct month. Confirm whether the selected previews are for the completed edition's recap or the upcoming edition when handing off the assets.

## 8. Handoff philosophy

- Keep all routines and recaps in this repo so they are accessible to future hosts and coding agents.
